\section{Agent-based modelling and simulation}
\label{sec:abms}
\slidesrc{ABMS: 1--21}

The third use of MAS (Section~\ref{sec:uses-of-mas}) is \textbf{simulation}: instead of
building agents that act in the real world, we build a population of (usually simple)
agents in an artificial world and observe what \emph{emerges} from their interaction. The
deck (also based on tutorials by S.~Coffman-Wolph, S.~Kaisler and G.~Madey) starts by
recalling the Macal and North quotes on ABMS as ``a third way of doing science''
(slides 2--3, see Section~\ref{sec:uses-of-mas}) and NetLogo (slide 4), then discusses
swarms and swarm intelligence, and finally ABMS proper.

\begin{nbsummary}[Agent-based modelling and simulation]
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Swarm}: a loosely structured collection of interacting agents (not
        necessarily identical) that contribute to and benefit from the group; better seen as
        agents exhibiting a \textbf{collective behaviour}. Examples: bees, ants, birds, fish,
        traffic, crowds, the immune system, even the mind (Minsky's society of mind).
  \item \textbf{Swarm intelligence} (Beni and Wang 1989; extended by Bonabeau, Dorigo,
        Theraulaz 1999): collective behaviour of decentralised, self-organised systems;
        simple agents, local (partly random) interactions, no central control, emergent
        ``intelligent'' global behaviour. Boids (Reynolds 1986).
  \item Advantages: \textbf{scalable}, \textbf{flexible}, \textbf{robust}.
  \item \textbf{ABMS}: computational methodology to create, analyse and experiment with
        artificial worlds of interacting agents; agents compete and/or collaborate towards
        individual and social goals; good simulation needs good science behind the model.
  \item Why: the real system is inaccessible, cannot be disturbed, has the wrong time scale,
        does not exist (anymore / yet); modelling forces hypotheses to be precise.
  \item Tools: \textbf{NetLogo} (turtles on patches, models library: e.g.\ Schelling's
        segregation), MASSIVE (crowds in films).
\end{itemize}
\end{nbsummary}
